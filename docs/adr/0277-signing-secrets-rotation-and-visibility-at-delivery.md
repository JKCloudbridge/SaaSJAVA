# ADR-0277: Signing, secrets, rotation, and visibility at delivery (S32)

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-23
- **Sprint:** S32
- **Related:** [ADR-0115](0115-the-record-access-model-and-how-it-reaches-a-statement.md), [ADR-0212](0212-the-secrets-store-envelope-encryption-and-the-keys.md), [ADR-0221](0221-the-inbound-flow-the-receiver-and-the-signature.md), [ADR-0274](0274-what-a-webhook-subscription-is-and-where-it-lives.md), [ADR-0275](0275-the-event-catalogue-and-the-payload-of-a-webhook.md)

## Decision

### Signing

1. **HMAC-SHA256 over `<timestamp>.<exact body bytes>`**, with the subscription's secret as the key, in lower case hexadecimal.
   The timestamp is whole seconds since 1970 and is also sent in a header, so a receiver that checks it against its own clock
   (the guide recommends **five minutes**) refuses a recorded delivery that is sent again later. The pure functions are in
   `WebhookSignatures`; `InboundSignatures` takes the same care in the other direction (the exact bytes, a window of time, a
   comparison that takes the same time whether it fails early or late: `MessageDigest.isEqual`, never `String.equals`).
2. **Headers:** `Webhook-Id` (the delivery identifier, which the receiver deduplicates by), `Webhook-Event`, `Webhook-Timestamp`,
   `Webhook-Signature` (`v1=<hex>`, one for every secret that signs, separated by a space), `Webhook-Attempt`. A change of the
   signed string would be a new scheme name (`v2=`), never a silent change of `v1`.
3. **The secret must be recoverable by the server** (a signature needs the key), so it is not a hash column: it is sealed in the
   secrets store under the organization's own scope with a name made of the subscription's identifier and the secret's number
   (`wh-<id>-<n>`, never a name a person typed). It is generated by the platform (32 random bytes, `whsec_` and the URL-safe
   text of them), **shown once** in the answer of the call that made it (never cached, never returned again by any call, never
   in a list, a log, an audit record or a metric), and wiped from memory after use.
4. **One class reveals it:** `WebhookSigner` (and nothing else; it also serves the test). The `SecretValue.reveal()` list of
   `SecretsArchitectureTest` changes by exactly that class.

### Rotation

5. **A new secret is current at once; the old one may keep signing for an overlap of up to 24 hours** (`overlapHours`, 0 to 24).
   While it overlaps, every delivery carries **two signatures** and the receiver accepts either, so it can change over without
   losing a delivery. A secret still overlapping from an earlier replacement ends when the next is made: at most two sign.
   **0 ends the old secret at once, which is the answer to a leaked secret:** it is removed from the store, so no delivery
   carries a signature of it from then on. The overlap can also be ended by hand (`secret/end-overlap`). A delivery whose
   signing finds no usable secret is not sent unsigned: it ends with a stable reason.

### Visibility at delivery

6. **An event knows nothing about who may see its record.** If a subscriber were simply told "record X changed" it would learn
   that records exist and that they changed, which the member the subscription stands for may not be allowed to know. So the
   question is asked again **when the delivery is made**, for the acting member, in the organization's own context, by
   `WebhookVisibility`, which asks the decision API (`Decisions`: the object permission, the licence, the record-level security
   of ADR-0115) and adds no rule of its own (an architecture test keeps `Records`, `BackgroundRecords` and `BackgroundWrites` out
   of the webhooks). Four answers: **send**; **`not_visible`** (a counted, visible skip); **`member_inactive`** (the subscription
   is stopped; the delivery is set aside as skipped and counted); **`object_not_listed`**.
7. **A skipped delivery names no record.** The delivery log shows the state and the reason and **no record identifier and no
   version** for a skip of the first two kinds, and no count, log line or metric ever holds one, so the log cannot be used to
   find out that a record exists.
8. **A removal is told only to a member who could read every record of the object** (public read, or view all): a removed record
   is out of the record store's reach for the owner and the shares that could once have shown it to the member, so it is judged
   by the object alone, and any other member's subscription skips it. This errs on the side of telling less; the receiver of such
   a subscription finds the record gone the next time it fetches it.

## Consequences

- A webhook can never be a way to read around permissions: it carries no value and no field name, and only for records the
  member can read now.
- A leaked signing secret is one call; a lost one is one call.
